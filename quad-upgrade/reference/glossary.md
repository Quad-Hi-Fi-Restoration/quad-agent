# Plain-English glossary

Use these definitions when a reader is new to electronics. Introduce only the terms needed for the current task, and explain what a part does in this circuit rather than assuming the name is enough.

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Board / PCB** | The board that holds the components and the copper paths joining them. |
| **Circuit reference / ref** | The label printed on a diagram or board, such as `C77` for a capacitor or `R34` for a resistor. The same label helps match the written instructions to the part. |
| **Capacitor** | A part that stores electrical charge. Its value is often marked in µF, nF, or pF. Some types have a positive and negative lead and must face the right way. |
| **Resistor** | A part that limits or sets the amount of current or voltage in a circuit. Its value is often marked in Ω, kΩ, or MΩ. |
| **Diode** | A part that mainly lets current flow in one direction. Its direction and replacement type can matter. |
| **Transistor** | A part used to switch or amplify electrical signals. Similar-looking types can have different pin arrangements. |
| **IC / integrated circuit** | A small packaged circuit with several pins. An op-amp is one kind of IC used to amplify or shape audio signals. |
| **Value** | The amount a part is designed to have, such as 100 µF or 10 kΩ. It does not specify all the other properties a replacement needs. |
| **Voltage rating** | The highest voltage a component is designed to withstand. A replacement must meet the source-backed requirement; a higher rating alone does not prove it fits or is suitable. |
| **Tolerance** | How far a part's actual value may vary from its marked value, usually shown as a percentage. It matters in audio filter networks. |
| **Lead spacing / pitch** | The distance between a component's metal leads. It must fit the holes or pads on the board. |
| **Polarity / orientation** | Which way round a part is installed. Reversing a polarised part can damage the component or the equipment. |
| **Schematic / circuit diagram** | A drawing of how the electrical circuit connects. It is not a picture of where each part sits on the board. |
| **BOM** | Bill of materials: a list of parts and their values for a particular model, board, and job. |
| **Recap** | Replacing some or all capacitors. A recap is restoration when parts are replaced to the documented original function; it may become a modification if values or circuit behavior change. |
| **Modification / mod** | A deliberate circuit change that alters how the equipment works. Keep it separate from like-for-like restoration. |
| **Provisional / unverified** | A sourced value that has not been checked against a physical unit. Confirm it against the user's board before ordering or fitting. |
| **Conflict** | Two sources give different or internally inconsistent information. Do not choose a value until the discrepancy is resolved. |

If the user does not know a term, explain it briefly before asking them to identify, measure, remove, or replace a part.
