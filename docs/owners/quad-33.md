## What it is
The Quad 33 is a transistor preamplifier, which Quad called a control unit. It chooses your source and sets volume and tone. It is usually paired with the Quad 303 power amplifier. Inside, most of its circuits are on small boards that plug in.

## Write these down
- The serial number.
- What you plug into it: turntable (and which cartridge), tuner, CD player, streamer.
- Which power amplifier you use it with.
- Anything already done to it, such as an earlier repair.

## What a recap does
Old capacitors dry out, and that can make the sound dull, noisy or crackly. A recap replaces them with new ones. DADA's kit also swaps the small transistors for quieter modern ones.

## Optional extras
- **A stronger power supply.** DADA's kit raises the internal supply so the 33 has more headroom and lower distortion. One resistor on the phono board has to change at the same time, so a technician does both together.
- **Less gain for modern sources.** CD players and streamers are much louder than 1960s sources. This change lets them plug into the radio inputs without overloading. Skip it if you use Quad tuners, or if your CD player goes into the tape input.
- **Turntable level after that change.** If the turntable becomes too quiet, the fix is usually just moving the plug-in phono board to another position. The Quad instruction book shows how.

## Good to know
- Crackles or a channel cutting out when you press buttons are usually dirty switch or connector contacts, not the recap. A technician can clean them.
- If hum changes as you turn the filter control, the 33 is probably too close to the power amp's transformer. Move them apart.
- The way mono and stereo work depends on which input you use. That is how Quad designed it, not a fault.
